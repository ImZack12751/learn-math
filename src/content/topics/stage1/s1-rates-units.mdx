<WhyItMatters>

Measurements only make sense with their units, and the same length can be written in kilometres, metres or millimetres. Converting between them correctly is a basic skill in science, technology and everyday life.

A rate, such as a speed in kilometres per hour, compares two different kinds of quantity. Rates are direct proportion in action, and they lead later to gradients and, in calculus, to rates of change.

</WhyItMatters>

<Explanation>

<Section id="converting-units" title="Converting metric units">

Metric units go up in powers of ten, the same pattern as <Term id="place-value" />:

$$
1 \text{ km} = 1000 \text{ m} \qquad 1 \text{ m} = 100 \text{ cm} \qquad 1 \text{ cm} = 10 \text{ mm}
$$

$$
1 \text{ kg} = 1000 \text{ g} \qquad 1 \text{ litre} = 1000 \text{ ml}
$$

These are definitions of the units, so there is nothing to work out: they are the facts every conversion uses.

Converting to a smaller unit gives more of them, so multiply. Every kilometre is 1000 metres, so 3.5 km is

<Claim display tex="3.5 \times 1000 = 3500" />

metres. Converting to a bigger unit gives fewer of them, so divide. Every 100 cm makes a metre, so 425 cm is

<Claim display tex="425 \div 100 = 4.25" />

metres. To go across two steps, such as kilometres to centimetres, convert one step at a time.

<Misconception id="convert-wrong-direction" />

<Callout kind="note">

A quick check: a smaller unit should give a bigger number, and a bigger unit a smaller number. A length in millimetres is always a bigger number than the same length in metres.

</Callout>

</Section>

<Section id="speed" title="Rates and speed">

A <Term id="rate" /> says how much of one quantity there is for each unit of another. "Per" means "for each". The most common rate is <Term id="speed" />: the distance travelled in each unit of time. A car at 60 km/h covers 60 kilometres in each hour.

Speed, distance and time are in <Prereq id="s1-ratio-proportion">direct proportion</Prereq>: at a steady speed, twice the time means twice the distance. So:

$$
\text{distance} = \text{speed} \times \text{time}
$$

$$
\text{speed} = \text{distance} \div \text{time}
$$

$$
\text{time} = \text{distance} \div \text{speed}
$$

The units tell you which is which. Kilometres per hour is kilometres divided by hours. A cyclist who travels 50 km in 2 hours has speed

<Claim display tex="50 \div 2 = 25" />

kilometres per hour.

</Section>

<Section id="time-in-hours" title="Times in hours and minutes">

When a speed is in kilometres per hour, the time must be in hours. An hour has 60 minutes, so divide the number of minutes by 60:

Forty-five minutes is this many hours:

<Claim display tex="\frac{45}{60} = 0.75" />

Ninety minutes is this many hours:

<Claim display tex="\frac{90}{60} = 1.5" />

A car at 60 km/h for 1 hour 30 minutes travels

<Claim display tex="60 \times 1.5 = 90" />

kilometres.

<Practice />

<Takeaways>

- 1 km = 1000 m, 1 m = 100 cm, 1 cm = 10 mm, 1 kg = 1000 g, 1 litre = 1000 ml.
- To a smaller unit, multiply; to a bigger unit, divide. A smaller unit always gives a bigger number.
- A rate is an amount per unit of something else. Speed is distance per unit of time.
- distance = speed × time; speed = distance ÷ time; time = distance ÷ speed.
- With a speed in km/h, put the time in hours: divide minutes by 60. Never write 1 hour 30 minutes as 1.30 hours.

</Takeaways>
