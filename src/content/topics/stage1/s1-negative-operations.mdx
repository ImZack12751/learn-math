<WhyItMatters>

Negative numbers are only useful if you can calculate with them. Every equation you solve later, every graph that dips below the axis and every change that goes down depends on the four rules on this page.

Each rule here comes from a picture on the number line, so you can rebuild it whenever you are unsure, rather than having to remember it.

</WhyItMatters>

<Explanation>

<Section id="adding-and-subtracting" title="Adding and subtracting: moves on the line">

From <Prereq id="s1-integers-number-line" />, adding a positive number moves right along the number line and subtracting a positive number moves left. That stays true wherever you start, including below zero.

Start at $-5$ and add 8: move 8 to the right. The first 5 steps reach zero and the last 3 carry on past it:

<NumberLine from={-7} to={5} points={[-5, 3]} jumps={[[-5, 3]]} />

<Claim display tex="-5 + 8 = 3" />

Start at 3 and subtract 8: move 8 to the left. The first 3 steps reach zero and 5 more go beyond it:

<Claim display tex="3 - 8 = -5" />

The result of adding is called the <Term id="sum" /> and the result of subtracting is called the <Term id="difference" />.

</Section>

<Section id="subtracting-negatives" title="Adding and subtracting a negative number">

What could adding $-4$ mean? Think of money: a negative number is a debt. Being handed a debt of 4 pounds leaves you 4 pounds worse off. So adding a negative number is the same as subtracting its size:

<Claim display tex="6 + \left(-4\right) = 6 - 4 = 2" />

Now subtract a negative. Taking a debt of 4 pounds away from you leaves you 4 pounds better off. So subtracting a negative number is the same as adding its size:

<Claim display tex="3 - \left(-4\right) = 3 + 4 = 7" />

The pattern also shows it. Subtract smaller and smaller numbers from 3, and watch the answers:

<Claim display tex="3 - 2 = 1" />

<Claim display tex="3 - 1 = 2" />

<Claim display tex="3 - 0 = 3" />

Each time the number taken away goes down by one, the answer goes up by one. Continuing the pattern:

<Claim display tex="3 - \left(-1\right) = 4" />

<Callout kind="idea">

Two signs next to each other combine into one. Subtracting a negative means adding; adding a negative means subtracting.

$$
a - \left(-b\right) = a + b \qquad a + \left(-b\right) = a - b
$$

</Callout>

</Section>

<Section id="multiplying-negatives" title="Multiplying and dividing">

Multiplying a whole number counts repeated additions. Three lots of $-4$ is a debt of 4 taken three times:

<Claim display tex="3 \times \left(-4\right) = \left(-4\right) + \left(-4\right) + \left(-4\right) = -12" />

The result of multiplying is called the <Term id="product" />. Swapping the order of a product does not change it, so $\left(-4\right) \times 3$ is also $-12$. A positive times a negative is negative.

For a negative times a negative, use the pattern again. Multiply $-4$ by smaller and smaller numbers:

<Claim display tex="2 \times \left(-4\right) = -8" />

<Claim display tex="1 \times \left(-4\right) = -4" />

<Claim display tex="0 \times \left(-4\right) = 0" />

Each step down in the first number adds 4 to the answer. One more step continues the pattern:

<Claim display tex="\left(-1\right) \times \left(-4\right) = 4" />

<Claim display tex="\left(-2\right) \times \left(-4\right) = 8" />

So a negative times a negative is positive. On the number line, multiplying by a negative number turns you around to face the other way; doing it twice faces you forwards again.

<Misconception id="negative-times-negative-negative" />

Division undoes multiplication, so its signs follow the same rule. The result of dividing is called the <Term id="quotient" />. Because <Claim tex="\left(-3\right) \times 4 = -12" />:

<Claim display tex="-12 \div 4 = -3" />

<Claim display tex="-12 \div \left(-3\right) = 4" />

<Callout kind="idea">

When multiplying or dividing two numbers: same signs give a positive answer, different signs give a negative answer. Decide the sign first, then work with the sizes.

</Callout>

With more than two numbers, each pair of negatives makes a positive. So an even number of negative factors gives a positive answer, and an odd number gives a negative answer:

<Claim display tex="\left(-2\right) \times \left(-3\right) \times \left(-5\right) = -30" />

</Section>

<Practice />

<Takeaways>

- Adding moves right on the number line and subtracting moves left, whichever side of zero you start on.
- Adding a negative is subtracting its size: $a + \left(-b\right) = a - b$.
- Subtracting a negative is adding its size: $a - \left(-b\right) = a + b$.
- Multiplying or dividing two numbers: same signs give a positive answer, different signs give a negative answer.
- With several factors, an even number of negatives gives a positive product and an odd number gives a negative product.

</Takeaways>
