<WhyItMatters>

Amounts bigger than one whole are often given as mixed numbers: two and a half hours, one and three quarter litres. To calculate with them reliably you need to move between mixed numbers and improper fractions, and to know which form suits which job.

This topic brings together everything so far on fractions: equivalent fractions, simplifying, adding and multiplying.

</WhyItMatters>

<Explanation>

<Section id="two-forms" title="Two ways to write the same amount">

A <Term id="proper-fraction" /> such as $\frac{3}{4}$ is less than one whole. An <Term id="improper-fraction" /> such as $\frac{11}{4}$ is one whole or more: its numerator is at least its denominator.

Eleven quarters is two whole lots of four quarters, with three quarters left over. Written as a whole number and a proper fraction side by side, that is a <Term id="mixed-number" />:

<FractionBar bars={[[4, 4], [4, 4], [3, 4]]} />

<Claim display tex="\frac{11}{4} = 2\frac{3}{4}" />

A mixed number means a sum: the whole number plus the fraction.

<Claim display tex="2\frac{3}{4} = 2 + \frac{3}{4}" />

Mixed numbers are easier to picture: two and three quarters is clearly between 2 and 3. Improper fractions are easier to calculate with.

</Section>

<Section id="converting" title="Converting between the two">

**Mixed number to improper fraction.** Each whole is as many pieces as the denominator. So multiply the whole number by the denominator, then add the numerator:

<Claim display tex="2\frac{3}{4} = \frac{2 \times 4 + 3}{4} = \frac{11}{4}" />

<Misconception id="mixed-to-improper-add" />

**Improper fraction to mixed number.** Divide the numerator by the denominator. The quotient is the number of wholes, and the remainder is the number of pieces left over:

<Claim display tex="23 = 4 \times 5 + 3" />

<Claim display tex="\frac{23}{5} = 4\frac{3}{5}" />

</Section>

<Section id="calculating" title="Calculating with mixed numbers">

The safe method for every operation is: change each mixed number to an improper fraction, calculate as in <Prereq id="s1-add-subtract-fractions" /> or <Prereq id="s1-multiply-divide-fractions" />, then change the answer back to a mixed number.

<Claim
  display
  tex="1\frac{1}{2} + 2\frac{2}{3} = \frac{3}{2} + \frac{8}{3} = \frac{9}{6} + \frac{16}{6} = \frac{25}{6} = 4\frac{1}{6}"
/>

For addition and subtraction you can also add the whole numbers and the fractions separately, because a mixed number is a sum. For multiplication you cannot:

<Misconception id="multiply-mixed-parts-separately" />

The correct product, through improper fractions:

<Claim
  display
  tex="1\frac{1}{2} \times 2\frac{1}{3} = \frac{3}{2} \times \frac{7}{3} = \frac{21}{6} = \frac{7}{2} = 3\frac{1}{2}"
/>

</Section>

<Practice />

<Takeaways>

- A mixed number is a whole number plus a proper fraction: $2\frac{3}{4}$ means $2 + \frac{3}{4}$.
- Mixed to improper: multiply the whole number by the denominator, add the numerator, keep the denominator.
- Improper to mixed: divide the numerator by the denominator; the quotient is the whole number, the remainder is the new numerator.
- To calculate, change to improper fractions first, then change the answer back.
- Never multiply the whole numbers and the fractions separately.

</Takeaways>
