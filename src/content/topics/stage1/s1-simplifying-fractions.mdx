<WhyItMatters>

The same amount can be written as many equivalent fractions. Writing it with the smallest possible numbers makes it easier to understand, to compare and to calculate with, and it is how answers are expected to be given.

Simplifying is also the first place you cancel common factors, a move you will make constantly with algebraic fractions in Stage 3.

</WhyItMatters>

<Explanation>

<Section id="simplifying" title="Dividing by a common factor">

From <Prereq id="s1-fractions-equivalent" />, dividing the top and the bottom of a fraction by the same number gives an equivalent fraction. A fraction is in its <Term id="simplest-form" /> when there is no number other than 1 left that divides both.

To get there in one step, divide by the <Term id="hcf" /> of the numerator and the denominator. For eighteen twenty-fourths, the HCF of 18 and 24 is 6:

<Claim display tex="\frac{18}{24} = \frac{18 \div 6}{24 \div 6} = \frac{3}{4}" />

<FractionBar bars={[[18, 24], [3, 4]]} />

You do not have to find the HCF first. Dividing by any common factor is correct; you just keep going until no common factor is left:

<Claim display tex="\frac{18}{24} = \frac{9}{12} = \frac{3}{4}" />

<Callout kind="warning">

Stopping early, at $\frac{9}{12}$, gives the right value but not the simplest form. Before you finish, check that the top and the bottom have no common factor.

</Callout>

<Misconception id="divide-one-part-only" />

For larger numbers, prime factorisations show every common factor at once. Each prime shared by the top and the bottom can be cancelled:

<Claim
  display
  tex="\frac{84}{126} = \frac{2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 7}{2 \times 3 \times 3 \times 7} = \frac{2}{3}"
/>

</Section>

<Practice />

<Takeaways>

- A fraction is in its simplest form when its top and bottom have no common factor other than 1.
- Divide the top and the bottom by their HCF to simplify in one step; dividing by smaller common factors works too, if you keep going.
- Always divide both the numerator and the denominator by the same number.
- To write one amount as a fraction of another, put the part over the whole, in the same unit, then simplify.

</Takeaways>
